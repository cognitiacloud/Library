# Research: DealerMine CRM / BDC

STATUS: INTERNAL — RESEARCH NOTES. Compiled 2026-07-03 from public sources.

Scope note: research target given as "dealermine.com". IMPORTANT DISAMBIGUATION —
the automotive CRM/BDC company DealerMine Inc. (Saint John, New Brunswick, a
division of Quorum Information Technologies, TSXV: QIS) markets at
**dealerminecrm.com** and **moriautomotive.ai**. The domain **dealermine.com**
belongs to a *different, unrelated* company ("Dealermine Corporation",
Clackamas, Oregon — a web service for liquidating slow-moving parts
inventory/obsolescence). All findings below concern the Saint John CRM/BDC
company unless flagged. Do not cite dealermine.com content as the CRM vendor's.

Method note: dealerminecrm.com, quoruminfotech.com, moriautomotive.ai and
globenewswire.com blocked direct page fetches from this environment (403 at
CDN/proxy), so details were assembled from search-index extracts of those
pages plus press releases. Every claim below traces to a URL in ## Sources.
Anything unverifiable is tagged NEEDS_EXTERNAL_RESEARCH.

## Company snapshot

- Founded 2001, Saint John, New Brunswick. Service CRM + Sales CRM + managed
  BDC (Business Development Centre) / call-centre services for franchised
  dealerships across Canada and the US.
- Acquired by Quorum Information Technologies (share purchase agreement dated
  2018-10-31, announced 2018-11-05). Acquisition took Quorum from 447 to 860+
  rooftop customers; DealerMine management stayed in Saint John.
- Sibling Quorum brands: Quorum DMS (XSellerator), Autovance (digital
  retailing/desking), Accessible Accessories. DealerMine is Quorum's CRM+BDC
  pillar — "drives revenue into the critical sales and service departments."
- Scale signals (Quorum public filings/releases, 2025): BDC revenue CAD ~$2.6M
  in Q1 2025 (+4% YoY) and ~$2.7M in Q3 2025 (+10% YoY); company-wide organic
  recurring-revenue growth 2–3% per quarter; Cash EBITDA $1.0M→$1.4M→$1.5M
  across Q1–Q3 2025 under an "Adjusted EBITDA improvement plan." Read: a
  small-cap parent optimizing profitability, not blitz-scaling R&D.
- Customer base: search-index extracts cite "over 500 dealership customers in
  North America." NEEDS_EXTERNAL_RESEARCH: current audited rooftop count and
  Canada/US split.
- Positioning taglines: "Know More, Book More, Sell More"; "The 360°
  Automotive CRM"; BDC-professional DNA ("designed by BDC professionals for
  BDC professionals").

## Products covered

### 1. DealerMine Service CRM (flagship)

- Positioning: single sign-in platform that "analyzes data, uncovers
  opportunities, and manages customer communications" for the service
  department; the revenue engine for fixed ops.
- Key features (verified from vendor pages/search extracts):
  - Data mining of the service/ownership base to find revenue opportunities:
    declined maintenance, deferred repairs, refusals ("turn declined
    maintenance, deferred repairs and refusals into service revenue").
  - Campaign engine: maintenance reminders (service due soon), outstanding
    recall follow-ups, satisfaction inquiries, declined-work follow-ups,
    appointment confirmations, missed-appointment rebooking.
  - Multi-channel outreach: phone, email, text/SMS, direct mail, ringless
    voice drop, surveys — "while ensuring privacy compliance" (CASL-aware
    framing for Canada).
  - Recall Integration: proactively checks for outstanding recalls during the
    appointment-booking call and notifies the customer in the same
    interaction — raises recall completion rates.
  - Online Scheduling: customer self-service appointment booking from any
    device, any time.
  - Telephony: integrated BDC telephony (call handling inside the CRM).
- Headline claim: dealers realize an **average 18% increase in customer-pay
  service revenue**.
- Pricing: not public. NEEDS_EXTERNAL_RESEARCH: per-rooftop/per-seat pricing,
  contract terms.
- Strengths: 20+ years of service-department muscle memory; the
  service-appointment campaign playbook (due/declined/recall/no-show) is the
  most complete in the Canadian market; OEM data pipes (GM Canada Dealer Data
  Share approved vendor since 2020-02).
- Weaknesses/complaints: entirely dependent on franchise DMS repair-order
  data — thin value for independents with no service department or with
  outsourced service; no public per-action evidence trail behind the "18%"
  aggregate claim; almost no third-party review footprint (Gartner Peer
  Insights page exists but NEEDS_EXTERNAL_RESEARCH: actual review
  count/scores; no substantive G2/Capterra presence found).
- Canada relevance: very high — HQ in NB, GM Canada approved vendor,
  AutoCanada group relationship, BC-dealer case studies (Westwood Honda,
  Port Moody BC).

### 2. DealerMine Sales CRM (+ Next Generation Desking)

- Positioning: "full sales funnel management… robust data mining… real time
  notifications… automated follow ups"; "100% visibility — a 360° view of
  your entire sales operation in one click."
- Key features:
  - Sales CRM Dashboard centralizing four workstreams: **Traffic, Web Leads,
    Sales Appointments, Work Plans** — buyer's journey view from first
    contact to conversion, with traffic analysis.
  - Campaign management, prospecting, lead management, funnel-stage tracking,
    automated follow-up plans ("Work Plans" = per-salesperson task queues).
  - Next Generation Desking: fully managed rates, residuals, rebates,
    customized worksheets; mobile desking "designed for salespeople and
    managers." (Overlaps/feeds Quorum's Autovance Desk retailing product.)
  - DMS integrations beyond Quorum's own DMS (e.g., Auto/Mate announced a
    DMS integration with DealerMine's "360-degree CRM").
  - Mobile app exists (Dealermine CRM app, Apple App Store, Canada).
- Pricing: not public. NEEDS_EXTERNAL_RESEARCH.
- Strengths: sales and service in one vendor; GM Canada sales-lead data
  share; desking in the same tool as follow-up.
- Weaknesses: as a sales CRM it is a follower, not a leader — feature language
  (funnel, work plans, notifications) is table-stakes vs VinSolutions/
  DealerSocket/Tekion; no public AI-native lead handling for *sales* leads
  comparable to its service-side AI; "traffic desk" mechanics (up-board,
  SLA timers, source accountability) are not visibly first-class.
- Canada relevance: high (GM Canada program is Canada-specific).

### 3. Managed BDC / call-centre services (the moat)

- Positioning: "a complete BDC solution managed by us and customized for
  you" — DealerMine runs the phones as an outsourced service from Saint John,
  on top of its own CRM software.
- Verified mechanics:
  - Claims **99.9% uptime** and **100+ calls per agent per day**.
  - Sales BDC, Service BDC, e-messaging/chat handling, and (since 2021)
    **collision-centre BDC**.
  - AutoCanada partnership: dedicated BDC facility for AutoCanada's Canadian
    dealerships (2019-01, initial staff of 85); expanded 2020-10 to manage
    AutoCanada's chat/electronic messaging (+10% headcount); expanded
    2021-08 to all AutoCanada collision centres.
  - Custom management reporting: built Westwood Honda a **"Lost Sales by BDC
    Associate"** report used for coaching; case study narrative = evolving a
    BDC from "call-and-book receptionist model" into a "Sales and Marketing
    BDC."
- Pricing: not public; economically it is a labour/services line (Quorum
  reports "BDC revenue" separately, ~$10-11M/yr run-rate). NEEDS_EXTERNAL_
  RESEARCH: per-seat / per-appointment pricing model.
- Strengths: real humans making outbound calls at scale is the hardest thing
  for dealers to self-build; enterprise anchor client (AutoCanada, one of
  Canada's largest public dealer groups); software+service bundle locks in.
- Weaknesses: labour-cost economics (low growth: +4–10% YoY); doesn't scale
  down gracefully to a 30-car independent lot; per-associate accountability
  reporting exists but appears bespoke ("built a custom report") rather than
  productized; outcome attribution is report-level, not receipt-level.
- Canada relevance: very high — the BDC service is physically Canadian and
  Canadian-dealer-group anchored.

### 4. MORI AI voice agent (moriautomotive.ai)

- Positioning: "#1 AI Voice Agent for Dealerships" — "answers every call
  instantly, engages customers in natural conversation, and books service
  appointments automatically," 24/7.
- Verified mechanics:
  - Handles the service-appointment call end-to-end and books in real time.
  - Call triage + warm transfer to the right team member.
  - Deployment modes: all inbound calls, after-hours only, overflow, or
    specific departments; "works with any phone system that can forward
    calls"; setup coordinates call routing with dealer IT.
  - Brand-tailored persona ("meets you at any stage of your AI journey").
  - Marketed as protecting revenue during peak hours / after hours / staff
    unavailability.
- Pricing: not public. NEEDS_EXTERNAL_RESEARCH: pricing, languages supported
  (French for Canada?), sales-call (vs service-call) capability, live booking
  write-back scope outside DealerMine's own scheduler.
- Strengths: credible AI story bolted onto a real BDC operation — "AI answers,
  humans escalate" hybrid ("Navigating the Crossroads — BDC vs. AI" is their
  own framing); instant-answer coverage of the #1 dealer failure mode
  (missed service calls).
- Weaknesses: service-department centric; a separate brand/site from the CRM
  (integration depth outside Quorum stack unverified); crowded category
  (Numa, Voice.ai, Voiceflow, Impel all pitch the same call).
- Canada relevance: high (sold through DealerMine's Canadian base).

### 5. DealerMine AI Batch Texting

- Positioning: automate routine service outreach texting "while providing
  intelligent responses," freeing BDC staff to close and book.
- Verified mechanics:
  - Personalized batch SMS campaigns to service customers.
  - Inbound replies **categorized by intent** so BDC staff triage faster and
    know when to escalate to a human.
  - Real-time response tracking; built-in reporting on message performance,
    engagement trends, and booking rates.
- Headline claim: within four months, **18% increase in average maintenance
  sales per repair order** for dealers using it.
- Weaknesses: campaign-blast paradigm with AI reply-sorting layered on —
  not a full conversational agent per lead; consent/opt-out mechanics not
  publicly detailed (NEEDS_EXTERNAL_RESEARCH: CASL consent workflow specifics).

## What DealerOS should copy conceptually

1. **Service-appointment campaign taxonomy.** Their five recurring campaign
   reasons — service due soon, declined/deferred work, outstanding recall,
   appointment confirmation, missed-appointment rebooking — map directly onto
   DealerOS Module 8 (Service CRM / fixed ops bridge). Ship these as named,
   pre-built campaign types with per-type outcome reporting from day one.
2. **Recall-check-in-the-booking-flow.** Checking recalls *during* the
   appointment interaction (not as a separate campaign) is a high-trust
   upsell moment. DealerOS AI BDC (Module 6) should run an inventory/recall/
   declined-work context lookup inside every appointment-draft flow.
3. **"Lost Sales by BDC Associate" as a product, not a custom report.**
   Per-associate (and per-agent) accountability reporting is what made
   Westwood Honda's BDC coachable. DealerOS TMS traffic desk (Module 4)
   already plans follow-up accountability — productize per-actor lost-lead /
   lost-appointment reports (human AND AI actor rows in the same table).
4. **Hybrid AI + human escalation framing.** MORI's "AI answers every call,
   warm-transfers when needed, humans coach the edge cases" is the
   claim-safe way to sell AI BDC to skeptical dealers. Matches our "sales
   desk with a paper trail — not a magic chatbot" metaphor.
5. **Intent-categorized inbound replies.** Batch Texting's reply triage
   (intent buckets + escalation flags + booking-rate reporting) is exactly
   the Module 6/13 pattern: lead classification + intent detection feeding a
   human work queue.
6. **Deployment-mode flexibility for voice.** MORI's routing menu (all calls /
   after-hours / overflow / department-specific) is the right adoption ramp
   for our voice-agent connector — let dealers start with after-hours only.
7. **Multi-channel including unfashionable channels.** Direct mail and
   ringless voice drop persist because older service customers respond.
   Keep connector slots for them even if v1 is SMS/email-first.
8. **One headline economic metric.** "18% increase in customer-pay service
   revenue" is memorable and department-specific. DealerOS proof reports
   should anchor on one wedge metric (e.g., lead→appointment rate) — but
   ours must be receipt-backed, not asserted (see below).

## What DealerOS should do better

1. **Serve independents first.** DealerMine's engine runs on franchise DMS
   repair-order data and OEM programs (GM Canada data share, AutoCanada).
   Independent used-car dealers — our wedge — are structurally underserved:
   no service department needed for our equity mining (Module 7 scores on
   finance term age, mileage, website behavior, inventory match instead).
2. **Software-scaled BDC instead of labour-scaled.** Their BDC grows ~4–10%
   YoY because it is people in Saint John. DealerOS's AI BDC does the instant
   response and drafting in software, with the dealer's own staff (or a
   partner call centre via connector) approving — gross-margin structure a
   services BDC cannot match, and it works for a 2-person independent.
3. **Sales-side traffic accountability as first-class.** Their sales CRM
   dashboard tracks Traffic/Web Leads/Appointments/Work Plans, but the
   up-board, SLA timers, no-show tracking, and sold/lost-reason discipline of
   a true traffic desk (our Module 4) are not visibly productized. Own the
   "traffic desk" word.
4. **Per-action attribution instead of aggregate lift claims.** They market
   "average 18% lift." We emit a proof receipt per lead, per appointment,
   per follow-up, per sold attribution — a dealer can audit *which* deals the
   system produced, monthly, in the proof report (Module 10/11).
5. **Transparent pricing.** No public DealerMine pricing anywhere. Publishing
   independent-dealer-sized pricing is itself a differentiator in this
   category.
6. **Modern review/community presence.** Their third-party review footprint
   is nearly empty. Early receipts-backed case studies + review presence is
   cheap ground to take.
7. **Unified brand and surface.** MORI lives on a separate site; desking
   overlaps Autovance; the CRM brand collides with an unrelated
   dealermine.com. DealerOS is one tenant-scoped platform with one login
   (Modules 1–15), connectors optional.
8. **Consent as data, not as a footnote.** They say "ensuring privacy
   compliance"; we store consent status per contact method with CASL/PIPEDA/
   PIPA(BC) basis recorded on every outreach receipt (Modules 2, 11, 14).

## How Cognitia proof receipts make our version harder to copy

- DealerMine's accountability artifacts are *reports* (dashboards, a bespoke
  "Lost Sales by BDC Associate" report, aggregate lift stats). Reports are
  assertions; receipts are evidence. Every DealerOS action — lead_received,
  ai_reply_drafted, human_approval_granted, appointment_confirmed,
  followup_sent, sold_marked — emits a Cognitia receipt with actor_type,
  policy_gate_result, consent_basis, payload_hash, timestamp, rollback and
  dispute paths (Module 11). A competitor can clone a dashboard in a quarter;
  retrofitting a receipt ledger under a 20-year-old CRM + human call centre
  means re-instrumenting every workflow and every BDC agent action.
- Their hybrid AI/human model has no public mechanism distinguishing "the AI
  booked it" from "a human booked it." Our receipts carry actor_id +
  agent_passport_id vs human_approver_id, so the per-associate coaching
  report they hand-built becomes an automatic, tamper-evident by-product.
- Their "18% lift" marketing claim is exactly the kind of claim we can make
  claim-safe: the monthly proof-backed report (Module 10) reconciles every
  attributed appointment/sale to receipts, so the number is auditable by the
  dealer — and disputable via the dispute path. That converts marketing into
  contract-grade accountability, which a managed call centre selling labour
  hours has strong incentives *not* to expose.
- CASL/PIPEDA enforcement risk is rising for outbound-heavy channels they
  lean on (batch SMS, ringless voice drop, direct mail). consent_captured
  receipts + policy gates before every external side effect turn compliance
  from a trust-us statement into queryable data — a structural moat for
  Canadian dealers.

## How Demandara integration makes our version more revenue-native

- DealerMine starts at the phone ring / DMS record: it optimizes the base a
  dealer already has. Demandara starts upstream — demand gen, SEO/AEO pages,
  campaigns, lead forms — and pushes leads into DealerOS via
  `POST /api/demandara/leads` with UTM/source attribution intact (Modules 9,
  10). DealerMine has no demand-generation engine at all; the dealer must buy
  websites/advertising elsewhere and attribution dies at the CRM boundary.
- Closed loop: DealerOS returns outcomes (`GET /api/demandara/outcomes`,
  `/revenue-attribution`) so Demandara reallocates spend to the campaigns
  that produce *sold* receipts, not form fills. DealerMine's campaign
  reporting stops at "booking rates" (Batch Texting) — no lead-to-sale spend
  feedback exists in their public product.
- Their strongest play — the service-to-sales BDC — maps to our
  service_to_sales_opportunity_created work event, but Demandara then runs
  the follow-up sequence (Sales Closer) with appointment drafts and human
  approval, and the monthly proof-backed marketing report shows the dealer
  one funnel from ad impression to signed deal, receipt-chained end to end.
- The MORI equivalent in our stack is a voice-agent *connector* (Module 12)
  feeding the same lead pipeline as web/SMS — every booked call becomes an
  appointment_confirmed receipt attributable to a Demandara campaign. Voice
  is a channel, not a separate product/brand/site.
